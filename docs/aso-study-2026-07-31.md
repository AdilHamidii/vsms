## 1. The five things that decide this

**1. Search is your entire acquisition channel.** 20,884 impressions over 15 days, **100% from App Store search** — zero browse, zero referral, zero ads. All 143 downloads likewise. **[ASC]** There is no other channel diluting the result, which makes ASO unusually high-leverage here *and* means a mistake has nowhere to hide.

**2. Your indexed surface is completely full, and contains no email term.** **[ASC]**

| Field | Live value | Used |
|---|---|---|
| Name | `vSMS: Temp Number, Receive SMS` | **30/30** |
| Subtitle | `Second Phone Number & eSIM` | 26/30 |
| Keywords | `burner,otp,code,verification,virtual,disposable,privacy,private,text,online,data,temporary,anonymous` | **100/100** |
| Localizations | **en-US only** | 1 of a possible 10 in the US storefront |

The words *mail* and *email* appear in none of them. The app **cannot currently rank for any email query at all** — not "temp mail", not "burner email", not "disposable email".

**3. The bottleneck is the search result, not the product page.** US funnel over 6 matched days: 7,107 impressions → 174 taps (**2.45% tap-through**) → 119 installs (**68.4% tap→install**). **[ASC]** A page converting two-thirds of visitors is doing its job. You are losing people at the moment they see your icon, title and first screenshots in the results list — which is exactly what the new screenshots can fix.

**4. Non-US impressions convert at literally zero.** 2,629 impressions outside the US produced **0 taps and 0 downloads**. **[ASC]** With an en-US-only listing, that is a strong signal you are being shown abroad and rejected on sight.

**5. Ratings, not keywords, cap your position — and the pool that can leave one is tiny.** The US storefront shows **0 ratings**; globally you have **3 reviews, all 5★, from France and Poland**. **[ASC]** The review prompt fires from a user's *second* delivered code, and only **7 users in the app's entire history have ever reached two codes**. **[DB]** Those 7 produced 3 reviews — a ~43% hit rate. **The prompt is working; the eligible pool is the constraint.**

## 2. The strategic call: "burner email", not "temp mail"

Your ASO tool makes "temp mail" look like the obvious target — popularity **78 US / 83 UK / 72 CA / 62 AU / 71 DE**, difficulty 34–39, "Sweet Spot" in all five. **[tool]** It scores higher than "temp sms" in every one of those countries.

**Chase the cluster, not the head term.** Three reasons:

**It is the most contested term in the category.** At least 11 iOS apps are literally named "Temp Mail …", the largest carrying **20,124 US ratings**. **[web — iTunes Search API]** You have 0 in the US. Difficulty 34–39 is a relative score; against that rating mass it is not winnable this quarter.

**The adjacent terms are ~1000× softer for the same intent.** Exact-match-named incumbents for the *burner/disposable* framing carry **1, 4, 24 and 118 ratings**. **[web — iTunes Search API]** Demand for "burner email" is **27,100/mo US**. **[web — Semrush US database]** That is real volume with almost no defence.

**The head term brings the wrong expectation.** The temp-mail category norm is a *free, unlimited, no-signup* inbox. Your free tier is 3 addresses/day on outlook/hotmail, then 1 credit. **[DB]** A user arriving from "temp mail" onto a credit paywall bounces — and Apple weights per-query conversion, so ranking badly there actively hurts you.

**One caution on the volume data.** The enormous web volumes for "temp mail" (1,220,000) are from the **India** database, and 246,000 from **Bangladesh** — not the US. **[web — Semrush]** Web volume ≠ App Store demand. Apple's popularity score *is* storefront-scoped, so US demand is real; it is simply not the 1.2M the raw web figure suggests.

**The genuinely uncontested position is the combination.** Only one iOS app was found doing temp number **and** temp email in a single listing — and it carries **16 ratings**. **[web — iTunes Search API]** That is the opening.

## 3. What the subtitle is currently costing you

`Second Phone Number & eSIM` spends your **second-heaviest indexed field** on two dead assets:

- **"Second Phone Number"** targets the single most contested and most wrong-intent cluster in the category — TextNow (913,221 ratings), Text Me (667,853), Text Free (594,098). **[web — iTunes Search API]** Those users want a permanent line they can *send* from. You rent a number for 8 minutes and cannot send. Unwinnable and, if won, worthless.
- **"eSIM"** is a product you paused yesterday. **[DB — `esim_paused = true`, 0 of 1,081 plans active]**

Replacing this line is the highest-leverage single change available.

## 5. Localization — the multiplier you are not using

This is the largest structural opportunity, and it converts a zero-sum choice into an additive one. Right now every new keyword must *displace* an existing one, because all three fields are full.

**Apple indexes every localization listed for a storefront, for all users there, regardless of their device language.** Each adds a full **160 characters** (30 name + 30 subtitle + 100 keywords).

Per Apple's own localization table **[web — developer.apple.com]**:

| Storefront | Default | Additional languages you could fill |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | English (U.S.) | **Spanish (Mexico)**, French, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, Korean, Arabic, Chinese (Simp./Trad.), Vietnamese — **9** |
| United Kingdom | English (U.K.) | none |
| Canada | English (Canada) | French (Canada) |
| Australia | English (Australia) | English (U.K.) |
| Germany | German | English (U.K.) |

**Do these in order:**

1. **Spanish (Mexico)** — a second full keyword field **in the US storefront**, where 73% of impressions and 100% of downloads originate. **[ASC]** Fill the hidden keyword field with the English terms you cut for space (`generator`, `throwaway`, `address`, `second`, `minute`, `trash`, `burner`). Highest single ROI available.
2. **English (U.K.)** — the additional supported language for most non-English storefronts worldwide (Germany, France, Spain, Brazil, Mexico, Switzerland…) and the *default* in India. One localization, indexed almost everywhere.
3. **English (Canada)** and **English (Australia)** — defaults in those storefronts only.
4. **German** — 5th market; a draft is in §9.

⚠️ **Two corrections to widely-repeated ASO advice**, both checked against Apple's table: adding en-GB/en-AU/en-CA does **nothing** for the US storefront (they are not US additional languages — the US extras are the nine non-English locales above). And India's default is English (U.K.), not Hindi.

⚠️ **The visible fields stay visible.** A user whose device is set to Spanish will *see* the Spanish (Mexico) name and subtitle. Write those properly; keyword-stuff only the hidden 100-character field. Nonsense visible metadata is precisely what Guideline 2.3.7 rejects.

## 6. Screenshots — you are redoing these, so this matters most

Your funnel says tap-through (2.45%) is the constraint and the product page (68.4%) is not. **[ASC]** In search results a user sees only the **icon, name, subtitle and first 1–3 screenshots**. That is the entire battleground.

- **Screenshot 1 must show both products.** Today nothing outside the app says you do email. If the listing starts claiming email, the first frame has to prove it or the claim reads as noise.
- **Lead with the free tier.** "3 free email addresses a day" is true **[DB]**, concrete, and directly answers the free-inbox expectation that would otherwise bounce temp-mail traffic.
- **Caption text should carry your keywords semantically.** Apple's indexing of screenshot text is disputed and I would not bet on it — but well-written captions cost nothing and help conversion regardless.
- **No prices, no "#1", no "best".** Guideline 2.3.7 names prices and unverifiable claims in screenshots specifically.
- **Remove eSIM imagery entirely while paused.** Showing a purchasable feature that returns zero plans is live 2.3 exposure. **[DB]**

The description is **not indexed by Apple** (unlike Google Play), so it is a pure conversion asset. Do not stuff it. But three things must change:

1. **"Auto-refund in credits if no code arrives within 20 minutes" is wrong.** The SMS window has been **8 minutes** since 2026-07-20: every order through that date ran 1200s, and all 92 orders since ran 480s. **[DB]** Same error in `docs/help.md` and `docs/app-store-listing.md`. 2. **The credit packs are now accurate but incomplete.** After today's price change the US bills $2.99 / $5.99 / $12.99 — matching the description — but the **$24.99 (60) and $59.99 (150)** packs are missing entirely. **[ASC]**

**Also fix the App Review notes.** They currently tell the reviewer they get 1 free credit and should try Discord + Indonesia. The signup grant is now **3 credits** and discord/id costs **4** **[DB]** — a reviewer following those instructions cannot complete the flow. That is a rejection risk that has nothing to do with keywords.

## 11. How to measure this

Apple exposes **no per-query search terms** — `Source Info` is empty on all 650 analytics rows. **[ASC]** So attribution must come from before/after comparison, not query data.

**Baseline, 15 days to 2026-07-29 [ASC]:** 20,884 impressions (1,392/day) · US 73.1% · 2.45% US tap-through · 68.4% tap→install · 119 first-time downloads · 0 US ratings.

**Change one layer at a time**, or you will not know what worked:
- **Metadata (name/subtitle/keywords)** moves **impressions**. Watch impression volume by territory.
- **Screenshots and icon** move **tap-through**. Watch the impression→tap ratio, which is currently 2.45%.
- **Localization** moves **non-US impressions and their tap-through**, currently 2,629 and 0%.

Allow **7–14 days** after each change before reading the result; Apple re-indexes over days and your daily impressions are volatile (241 to 6,476 across the sample). Note that 1.5 shipped this morning, so the next fortnight carries a fresh-release bump that is not attributable to ASO.
